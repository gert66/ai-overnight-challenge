How the idea started

After that meeting, I started brainstorming with AI during a walk about a simple demonstrator. That entire first design took less than half an hour and happened entirely through Voice Mode on my phone. No laptop, no keyboard, just talking. The premise was: come up with five small but different software problems that could test whether an AI environment can genuinely work on its own.

AI and I ended up with five ideas: object tracking, a 2×2 Rubik solver, a Monte Carlo particle lab, 3D path planning and a maze-chase game. I then had AI turn those rough ideas into proper challenge descriptions and acceptance criteria. So the longer instructions that eventually drove the software builds were themselves largely designed by AI.

After that, the human starting instruction could essentially be very short: work through these challenges autonomously, test them, review them and deliver demonstrably working demos. The orchestrator handled the sequencing and control. Hermes and the other tool bridges made models and tools available when needed.

The base prompt, reduced to the core

“Work through these five software challenges fully autonomously. Create a good executable assignment and acceptance criteria for each challenge, build and test the solution, have the result independently reviewed, repair defects and deliver five demonstrably working browser demos.”

The detailed technical rules do not all have to live in the human prompt if the orchestrator already knows them as part of its standard workflow.
