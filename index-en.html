AI Overnight Challenge

What happens if you prepare five very different software problems, put a small computer in charge of an AI orchestrator, and then stop intervening for hours?

On 16-17 September 2026 we tested exactly that with colleagues. All five assignments ended as working, tested browser demos.

If you know nothing about the project

This did not start with months of programming. The idea for a stress test came up after a physics meeting, during a walk. In roughly twenty minutes of discussion by phone, five very different problems were chosen and the rules were defined.

Those ideas were turned into a challenge pack: each task described what had to be built and what counted as real success. After that, the human launch instruction was short. The orchestrator could divide the work, call AI models, have code written, run tests, ask for independent review, repair defects and record accepted steps in Git.

One important detail: Hermes and the other tool bridges made models and tools reachable. The orchestrator remained the traffic controller deciding which step was allowed to happen next. A model therefore could not simply declare its own work finished.

The core question

After one human launch instruction, can a small AI environment build, check and repair software independently for hours without someone continually telling it what to do?
