The architecture: from conversation to commit

From a high-level assignment in ChatGPT to code, objective proof, independent review and a commit. The orchestrator owns the state machine and the control flow; Brain, Worker and Reviewer supply the AI reasoning within bounded roles.

The
BrainThe Brain is the AI role that picks one small, bounded piece of work (a "batch") from the assignment each time — the planning role in the loop.
picks one bounded next batch of work at a time. The
WorkerThe worker is the AI that actually carries out one small piece of work: writing code, making changes, or setting up tests.
carries out that batch within the agreed scope. The orchestrator then collects objective proof (diff, changed files, test results). Only then does the
ReviewerThe reviewer is the second, independent AI that checks the result against the acceptance criteria, separate from the AI that built it.
— independent of the Worker — check whether the actual result meets the
acceptance criteriaThe concrete, measurable requirements a solution must meet before it counts as 'done'.
. Only a PASS is followed by a commit and the loop moving on to the next batch; a FAIL goes back to the Brain for repair; NEEDS_HUMAN pauses the system for a genuine human decision.

The workflow: plan, build, test, review, repair, record

This is the same loop as on How can AI do this itself?, but here with the exact roles: the Brain picked one bounded next batch, the Worker carried it out, the orchestrator collected objective evidence, and the Reviewer independently checked that evidence against the acceptance criteria. Only after a PASS did a commit follow — never based on a promise from the Worker itself.

Review, repair and completion

Tests, type checks, builds and, where possible, Playwright end-to-end tests determined whether a step could move on to the next phase. An AI that judges its own work misses things just as easily as a person proofreading their own text — that's why the Reviewer always checked the actual result, independent of the Worker that built it.

Hermes, the models and Git

Hermes and other tool connections made models and tools available whenever needed: the bridge that let the orchestrator reach the right AI models and tools. Brain and Reviewer ran on
ClaudeClaude is the AI model family that did the reasoning work for the Brain and Reviewer role in this challenge, and as one of the possible providers for the Worker role.
. Only the Worker was provider-selectable: automatic, Claude, Gemini 3.8 Flash or Codex. In automatic mode, simpler (LOW/NORMAL) work could go to Gemini 3.8 Flash; higher complexity and certain escalations went to Claude Sonnet. Git safety, evidence, review and commits always stayed with the orchestrator, regardless of which model the Worker used that time.

Every accepted batch got its own commit. That kept development reproducible, visible and recoverable: you can read back afterwards exactly what changed and when. Alongside the autonomous orchestrator, Desktop Commander also ran on the same VM: a separate tool bridge that let ChatGPT, only when explicitly asked, directly view and edit files, start or monitor processes and read logs — useful for management and diagnosis, but not part of the autonomous state-machine loop itself.

A concrete example in Git: the 3D Path Planner

You don't have to take our word for the architecture. For the 3D Path Planner, the build history can simply be read back. Every accepted step was recorded as a separate commit:

01-core-planner-and-scenes → 02-threejs-viewer-and-controls → 03-exploration-playback-and-drone-animation → 04-playwright-e2e-tests → 05-visual-polish-and-demo-readiness → 06-pwa-mobile-readiness → final audit and acceptance.
