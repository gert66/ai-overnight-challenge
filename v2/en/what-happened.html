Just tell me what happened

No technical knowledge needed. This is the story as it happened, from a meeting about AI to five working programs the next morning.

The story

  1. A meeting about AI
    After a meeting with physics colleagues about AI, Gert kept thinking about it. He wanted to show colleagues, on a Friday afternoon, what AI can actually do today, more than just asking a question and getting an answer.
  2. A walk
    During a walk he took out his phone and started talking out loud to an AI app, using its voice feature. No keyboard, no laptop, just a conversation on the go.
  3. About half an hour, no keyboard
    In that conversation, while he was walking, the idea for a demonstration took shape. Together with the AI he came up with five very different small software assignments, and the first setup was put in place. He didn't touch a keyboard the whole time.
  4. The assignments get worked out
    After that, the AI expanded the rough ideas itself: from short sketches into full assignments, with a clear description of what a good result would look like.
  5. A night of working independently
    While everyone slept, the computer worked through the assignments by itself: planning, building, testing, checking and, where needed, fixing, step by step, without anyone needing to step in.
  6. Five working programs
    The next morning, five different, genuinely working programs were ready. You can open them for yourself right below, on your phone too.

Why is this interesting?

This shows that AI can do more than just answer a question. If you describe an assignment clearly enough, AI can carry it out independently: making a plan, building something, testing it, having its own work checked and fixing mistakes, entirely by itself, without anyone needing to approve every step.
