How could AI do this itself?

For anyone curious how AI can work independently for a whole night. This is the approach in outline, no code, but with the key terms briefly explained if you're interested.

The idea in short

Picture a building project with three roles: a project lead who splits the work into small, manageable pieces, a builder who actually carries out each piece, and an independent inspector who checks every result before the project moves on. In this experiment, AI filled all three roles itself, all night long, without anyone needing to be present.

The cycle: plan, build, test, check, fix, record

  1. Plan
    An
    orchestratorThe orchestrator is the traffic controller: the system that splits the work into small steps, has each step tested and checked, and only moves on once a step is genuinely good enough.
    would pick one small, manageable piece of work from the assignment and hand it over as a clear
    promptA prompt is an instruction in plain language, written or spoken, that you give to AI, for example a task or question that should produce a result.
    .
  2. Build
    A building AI carried out that one piece of work: writing code, making changes, or setting up tests.
  3. Test
    Every piece of work was tested automatically, so there was demonstrable proof that it actually worked, not just a promise.
  4. Independent review
    A second AI, separate from the building AI, independently checked the result: does this really match what was asked for?
  5. Fix
    If something wasn't good enough, it went back: build again, test again, check again, until it was right.
  6. Record progress
    Every approved step was saved separately in
    GitGit is the logbook: a system that saves every change to the code, so you can always read back exactly what was changed and when.
    , so the whole night can be read back afterwards.

The human's role

Gert provided the five ideas by talking about them during a walk, using the voice feature on his phone. He set the boundaries and what a good result would look like. He didn't write a single line of code himself that night: his work shifted from writing code to formulating the assignment, the boundaries and what the result needed to meet.

What it couldn't do, and caveats

Not every result suits every purpose. The Electron Dose Lab demo, for example, is a physics research demo based on public NIST data: not a clinical device and not a clinically validated dosing system.
